CO2: 1958-03 to 2026-09, 823 monthly values (0 missing values interpolated); 315.7 ppm in 1958 to 426.1 ppm in 2026
Temperature anomalies: GISTEMP 1880-2025 (146 full years), GCAG 1850-2025 (176 full years); warmest year in GISTEMP: 2024 (1.28 C); correlation of the two sources over the common years: 0.993

Seasonal cycle (STL): peak-to-trough amplitude 5.9 ppm in the 1960s, 6.8 ppm in the 2010s; linear change 0.15 ppm per decade (95% CI 0.12 to 0.18)
The CO2 cycle peaks in month 5 (May) and bottoms out in month 10: this is consistent with Northern-hemisphere vegetation taking up CO2 in the growing season (a physical interpretation, not tested here).

Since 1975: 0.207 C per decade (95% CI with HAC 0.180 to 0.233); with the NOAA series (GCAG): 0.207 C per decade -> the two sources agree.

Piecewise-linear fit with one knot (best knot 1975): slope 0.036 C/decade before, 0.205 C/decade after; SSE falls by 63% versus one straight line.
Quadratic trend: curvature coefficient 0.0095 C per decade squared (HAC t = 8.8); positive curvature means accelerating warming.
The knot year is chosen from the data, so the evidence for a change in slope is stronger than a naive test suggests; the exact knot is uncertain.

Annual temperature anomaly: ADF with trend (H0 unit root) p = 0.978; KPSS with trend (H0 trend-stationary) p = 0.010
ADF does not reject a unit root and KPSS rejects trend-stationarity: both point to non-stationarity even around a linear trend. This fits either a stochastic trend or a trend that is not linear (the piecewise and quadratic fits above); with 146 annual observations these tests cannot tell the two apart, and the debate in the literature is unresolved.

Annual temperature anomaly vs log2(CO2), 1959-2025 (67 years)
  Levels regression: 2.74 C per doubling of CO2 (HAC 95% CI 2.50 to 2.97), R2 = 0.93, residual lag-1 autocorrelation 0.25
  First differences: 17.83 C per doubling (HAC 95% CI 10.96 to 24.71), R2 = 0.13
  Engle-Granger cointegration test (H0: no cointegration): statistic -5.36, p = 0.000
The levels regression has a high R2 (0.93) partly because both series trend, which can also happen between unrelated trending variables (spurious regression). Here the Engle-Granger test rejects 'no cointegration', so the long-run co-movement is not just a statistical artefact of two trends; but the test has its own size and power limits with 67 annual observations.
In first differences the R2 is only 0.13 and the coefficient is large and imprecise (CI 11 to 25): annual changes in log CO2 are small and nearly constant, so they carry little variation, while year-to-year temperature changes are dominated by natural variability (El Nino, volcanoes). The levels coefficient should not be read as the climate sensitivity: temperature also responds to aerosols, solar output and ocean heat uptake, and the response lags the forcing. The point of this exercise is statistical: trending series need differencing or cointegration analysis before any regression result is interpreted.

Caveats: statistical description only; no physical model, so nothing here measures climate sensitivity or attributes warming to causes; annual CO2 and
temperature regressions are affected by omitted forcings and lags; the knot of the piecewise model is data-chosen; Mauna Loa is one location (though it tracks
the global mean closely); temperature series are anomalies from reanalysis and station networks with their own uncertainty; the file's last values may be provisional.
