The posterior median of tau ranges from 0.6 (inverse-gamma(0.001, 0.001) on tau^2) to 5.2 (uniform(0, 30)), and the posterior mean of theta_1 from 8.3 to 11.2. The inverse-gamma(0.001) prior is often called 'non-informative' but here it puts most weight near tau = 0 and gives the strongest shrinkage (Gelman 2006 warns about this); the uniform prior gives the weakest.

Features the model does not reproduce: skewness of log wage. The observed wages are more negatively skewed than the normal model implies (a left tail of very low wages), so the normal-error assumption is imperfect; the coefficients are still informative but predictive intervals in the left tail would be too optimistic.

Caveats: (A) eight observations carry almost no information about tau, so results depend on the prior; the Gibbs sampler mixes slowly near tau = 0 and
modern samplers (Hamiltonian Monte Carlo with a non-centred parametrisation) work better; the normal-normal model assumes known standard errors.
(B) a normal linear model with constant variance for log wages; the checks above indicate which features it misses; priors are subjective choices;
the regression is descriptive (see project 21 for the endogeneity of education).
