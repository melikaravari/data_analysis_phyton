1390 Senate races 1914-2010; 1297 with a next-election result (outcome observed). Democrat won 750 (54%).

Main RD estimate (local linear, triangular kernel, MSE-optimal bandwidth)
  Conventional: 7.41 points (SE 1.46)
  Bias-corrected: 7.51 (robust SE 1.74); robust 95% CI [4.09, 10.92]; robust p = 1.6e-05
  Bandwidth 17.8 points; effective observations 360 left / 323 right of the cutoff
Interpretation: a party that barely wins a Senate seat gets about 7-8 percentage points more of the vote in the next election for that seat.

Density test for manipulation of the running variable (Cattaneo-Jansson-Ma): p = 0.38 (a small p-value would suggest sorting around the cutoff)
Races decided by less than 2 points: 102, of which won by the Democrat: 52 (binomial test p = 0.92)

Probability of winning the next election: RD effect 33 percentage points (robust p = 4.5e-06); winning once raises the chance of winning again by about this amount.

Caveats: the estimate is local, for races that were close, and applies to the party, not to the individual candidate (the winner may not run again,
and a seat may change hands for reasons unrelated to incumbency); the next election is six years later, so many things change; the sample
is small near the cutoff (about 700 effective observations); the running variable has a few repeated values; results for the House of
Representatives or other periods may differ; the bandwidth choice is data-driven but not unique.
