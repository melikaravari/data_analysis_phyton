With only 5 observations per person, the individual lines are noisy, and the mixed model compromises between the group and the individual.

Residual diagnostics (random-slope model): residual SD 23.5 ms; Shapiro-Wilk p < 0.001; skewness 0.17; the spread of the data grows over the days (SD of reaction times 32 -> 67 ms), which the constant residual variance does not capture; this is mostly due to the random slopes.

Caveats: 18 people, all professional drivers, so the variance parameters are imprecise and the findings may not apply to others; a linear trend over
days is assumed (the early days may include training effects and the late days may saturate); residual variance is assumed constant and errors
independent within a person (no autocorrelation structure was fitted); the random effects are assumed normal; statsmodels' MixedLM does not provide
Kenward-Roger or Satterthwaite degrees of freedom, so Wald intervals are slightly too narrow in such a small sample.
