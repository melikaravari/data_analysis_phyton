The 2SLS estimate with nearc4 is clearly larger than the OLS estimate (0.13 vs 0.08) but has a much wider confidence interval. Explanations: (i) measurement error
in schooling attenuates OLS; (ii) the IV estimates a local effect for men whose schooling responds to college proximity (often high-return, credit-
constrained men), not the average effect (LATE); (iii) the instrument may be imperfectly exogenous. These cannot be separated with these data.

Wu-Hausman test of exogeneity of education (nearc4 as instrument): statistic 1.17, p = 0.280  (low power here: IV is imprecise, so OLS cannot be rejected formally although the point estimates differ)
Over-identification test, nearc4 + nearc2: Sargan statistic 1.25, p = 0.264
Over-identification test, nearc4 + father's + mother's education: Sargan 2.11, p = 0.348
Parental education is a very strong instrument (F > 200) but is the least credible one: family background probably affects wages directly, so its estimate (about 0.10) is not trusted more than the college-proximity one.
Failing to reject is reassuring but not proof: the test is only valid if at least one instrument is valid, and has limited power.

After controlling for region and urban location: IQ difference 0.67 points (p = 0.321). Raw differences in ability and family background between near and far men suggest the instrument may not be randomly assigned;
the controls absorb part of this. This is the main threat to the IV result.

Caveats: the exclusion restriction (proximity affects wages only through schooling) cannot be tested directly; urban areas near colleges may have
different labour markets; the data are from 1976 for one cohort of men, so the result may not hold today or for women; the sample with parental
education is smaller and selected; IV estimates are local effects; confidence intervals are wide.
