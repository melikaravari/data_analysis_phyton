Best AIC: SEM (spatial error). The spatial dependence is better described as correlated unobserved factors across neighbours (spatial error) than as a direct spillover of consumption. The neighbours' price coefficient in the SLX model has t = 0.6, so there is no clear evidence of cross-border shopping effects with this sample of 48 states.

The indirect effect is the effect on a state's consumption of changes in the price in other states, passed through the spatial lag. It is conditional on the SAR model, which does not fit best (see the AIC); rho is only moderately precise.

Moran's I of residuals: OLS 0.291, SLX 0.299 (p = 0.001)

SLX 1985: own price -1.13 (SE 0.27), neighbours' price -0.06 (SE 0.48)
A positive and significant neighbour-price coefficient would fit cross-border shopping (residents buy more at home when neighbours are more expensive); in both years it is small and imprecise.

Caveats: 48 observations per year, so spatial parameters are imprecise; the cross-section is not a causal design (prices and taxes are set by states that
differ in unobserved ways, such as tobacco-growing states with low taxes); a panel with state fixed effects would be better; the results depend on the
Queen contiguity weight matrix (distance-based weights could differ); LISA p-values are not corrected for multiple testing; the data are from 1985 and 1995.
