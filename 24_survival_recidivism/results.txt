Weibull AFT model: time ratio for aid = 1.31 (95% CI [1.00, 1.72]): aid multiplies the time to re-arrest by this factor; Weibull shape = 1.40 (> 1 means the hazard rises over time).
(Only 26% of the men are re-arrested within the follow-up, so the model says little about the time after week 52.)

5-fold cross-validated concordance of the Cox model: 0.602 (std 0.031); 0.5 = no discrimination, 1 = perfect. The model ranks
who is re-arrested earlier only modestly better than chance.

Caveats: the aid group received an offer, so these are intention-to-treat effects (not everyone may have received or used the aid); the follow-up is only
one year, with 74% censored; re-arrest is a measure of detected crime, not of offending; the 1970s Maryland sample of men may not generalise; the original
study also recorded weekly employment, which is not in this version and would allow an analysis of the mechanism (aid may reduce employment).
